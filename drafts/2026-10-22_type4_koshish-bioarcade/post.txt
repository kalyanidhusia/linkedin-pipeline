I spent the last year building six browser games for Arkansas undergrads to learn transcription, translation, protein folding, phagocytosis, pharmacokinetics, and antibiotic resistance.

Not because games are fun (though they are), but because I needed a format NIH reviewers would accept. That constraint — 'one concept per game, defensible to a grant officer' — forced me to strip out every decoration, every detour, every tempting side-explanation.

What survived: a three-step loop (Learn → Play → Check) that takes under 6 minutes. The game mechanic is the concept. Polymerase Runner doesn't teach transcription through a quiz on top of Flappy Bird; the jump timing *is* base-pairing at the transcription zone.

Every game states its simplifications in-game. 'The ribosome does not actually look like a snake.' That line isn't humility — it's what makes the work defensible.

Open question I'm sitting with: can I add zero-PII telemetry so the data students generate becomes the dataset they analyze? A loop worth building if it holds up.
