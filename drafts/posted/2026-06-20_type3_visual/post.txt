I remember my first classification model with 99% accuracy. So proud! Then my mentor asked, "What about your minority class?" My heart sank. That 1% error was what I cared about; my model was just great at predicting abundant 'normal' cases.

It's easy to get excited by a big accuracy number. But in biology, we often hunt for rare events – a specific mutation or a tiny cell subpopulation. If your dataset is imbalanced, a model can achieve high accuracy by simply predicting the majority class.

That's why I always dive deeper. Instead of just accuracy, I check precision and recall for each class. It tells you if your model is actually finding what you need, not just what's easy. This lesson stuck with me!

Meanwhile, making dumb machines solve biological problems!
