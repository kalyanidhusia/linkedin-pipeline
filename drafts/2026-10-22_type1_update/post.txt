I spent way too much time last month waiting for an RNA structure prediction job to finish. Not ideal when you're trying to test a hypothesis before the next lab meeting.

The NuFold RNA Structure Database just dropped on bioRxiv, and it's basically "here are the structures, already computed" for a massive number of RNA sequences. Full-atom tertiary structures, precomputed, with confidence scores included. No more spinning up jobs and checking back in 6 hours.

What I like: they're removing the computational barrier for people who need structures *now* but don't have a cluster sitting around. The confidence metrics matter too — knowing when to trust a prediction vs. when to be skeptical saves a lot of downstream pain.

Still need to dig into how they handle edge cases, but this feels like the kind of resource that quietly becomes part of everyone's workflow.

If you work with RNA and haven't bookmarked https://nufold.kiharalab.org/ yet, might be worth a look.
