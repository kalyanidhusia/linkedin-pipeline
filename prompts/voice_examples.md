# Voice examples — Kalyani's LinkedIn style

These are paraphrased patterns from Kalyani's actual posts. Use them as a style reference, not as templates to copy.

## What her voice sounds like

- Opens with a personal observation or moment, not a thesis statement
- Uses "I" liberally — shares the journey, including dead ends
- Tagline: "...meanwhile, making dumb machines solve biological problems!"
- Often closes with an invitation: "let's grow together", "follow my learning journey"
- Comfortable being uncertain in public — "I'm still figuring this out"
- 90% buzzword-free — when she does use jargon, she explains it in the next breath
- Mixes culture and code — references TV shows, Indian context, Aikido, daily life
- Hashtags are spare and topical, never a wall

## Structural patterns she uses

**Pattern A — The "I noticed" hook:**
> "Reposting for better reach... | Hi everyone — I am looking for a new role as a computational biologist..."
(Direct, personal, ask-driven.)

**Pattern B — The pop-culture parallel:**
> "This reminds me of Chandler (from the tv show 'Friends') once got a fancy laptop and was so excited about it!"
(Lands the technical idea on a familiar reference.)

**Pattern C — The reflection:**
> "No matter how much we prepare and expect the unexpected, something is always going to be beyond our control. It's important to 'Learn' and 'teach' to handle..."
(Personal philosophy, no pretension.)

**Pattern D — The work update:**
> "In my current position at CHI, NIH, I am building tools for high-dimensional analysis of flow/mass cytometry and SomaScan data and establishing an analysis pipeline for spatial transcriptomics."
(Specific, technical, factual — saved for posts where credentials matter.)

## Tone phrases she uses

- "...meanwhile, making dumb machines solve biological problems!"
- "I share my learning journey..."
- "let's grow together"
- "90% buzzword-free"
- "follow me and let's grow together"

## What to avoid

- "🚀 Exciting news!" / "I'm thrilled to announce" / "game-changer" / "revolutionary"
- Claiming expertise she hasn't earned ("the definitive guide to...")
- Long paragraph blocks — LinkedIn rewards line breaks
- More than 4-5 hashtags
- Generic motivational closers ("the future is now!")
- Heavy use of em-dashes — Kalyani prefers regular punctuation
